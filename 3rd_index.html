Healthcare Capacity by State: Hospital Beds per 1,000 People (2022)

Geographic distribution is uneven. Beds concentrate in Peninsular Malaysia. East Malaysian states are under-served despite their large geographic size.

Which States Have the Most Public Hospital Beds? (2022)

Absolute counts mislead. Selangor leads in total beds, but Perak ranks unexpectedly high due to older established public hospitals.

Do Hospital Beds Keep Up with Population Size?

Beds are not keeping up. A widening gap between bed supply and population signals growing strain on public healthcare infrastructure.

MOH Hospitals Dominate Bed Provision Across All States (2022)

MOH dominates nationally. Federal policy decisions directly shape bed availability in every state, with non-MOH facilities playing a secondary role.

Healthcare Staffing Across Malaysian States (2022)

Staff distribution mirrors bed inequality. Healthcare workers concentrate in urban federal territories, leaving rural and East Malaysian states with fewer per capita.

Hospital Beds vs. Healthcare Staff by State (2022)

Only two states are well-resourced on both dimensions. W.P. Kuala Lumpur and Perak exceed the national median in both beds and staff per 1,000 population.

Healthcare Workforce Composition by State (2022)

Workforce composition varies by state. A skewed mix of worker types can create capability gaps even where total staff numbers appear sufficient.

Is Malaysia's Healthcare Workforce Growing Fast Enough? (2015 to 2022)

Workforce growth is the time-based test of whether staffing can keep pace with broader system pressure.

B40 Health Screenings Per 1000 Population (2019 to 2025)

This section turns from hospitals to prevention. Screening rates show how public health reaches people before acute care is needed.

Blood Donations per 10,000 People by State: 2012 vs 2022

Organ Pledges in Malaysia: 2012 to 2022

Organ pledges provide a national indicator of longer-term public willingness to engage with healthcare systems.

Is Malaysia Spending More on Health?

Nominal spending has risen, but per-capita growth is the real test. Flat per-capita figures would signal real-terms stagnation despite rising headline numbers.
