Inside Malaysia's Healthcare System

How many hospital beds do we have, and where? Are staff and infrastructure keeping pace with a growing population? And is care actually reaching the people who need it most? This is a data-driven walk through the state of the nation's health.

Part 1

Where the beds are

Hospital beds are the foundation of healthcare capacity. But they are not spread evenly. We begin with the map of bed density, then rank which states carry the most public beds.

Healthcare capacity by state

Hospital beds per 1,000 people, 2022. Darker means more beds relative to population.

Read this first. Bed density varies widely across states once you adjust for how many people live there.
Which states have the most public hospital beds?

Total public-sector hospital beds by state, 2022.

Raw totals tell a different story. The biggest states by population also hold the most beds — but volume is not the same as adequacy.
MOH hospitals dominate bed provision

Share of beds supplied by Ministry of Health vs. other providers, by state, 2022.

Public provision is the backbone. In almost every state the Ministry of Health supplies most beds — Kuala Lumpur is the lone exception.
Part 2

Keeping up with people

Capacity only matters relative to demand. Has bed supply grown alongside the population? And do better-staffed states also have more beds?

Do hospital beds keep up with population?

Indexed growth of beds vs. population, 2012–2022.

A widening gap. Population climbs steadily while bed supply lags behind.
Beds vs. staff, side by side

Hospital beds and healthcare staff per 1,000 people, by state, 2022.

Infrastructure and people move together — states strong in one tend to be strong in the other.
Part 3

The people behind the care

Beds need staff. This section maps where healthcare workers are, what kinds of roles make up the workforce, and whether it is growing fast enough for the country it serves.

Healthcare staffing across states

Healthcare staff density by state, 2022.

Staff cluster in the same places as beds, reinforcing regional divides.
Workforce composition by state

Share of each staff type, by state, 2022.

The mix of roles shifts from state to state, not just the totals.
Is the workforce growing fast enough?

Healthcare workforce vs. population growth, 2015–2022.

Good news here. The workforce grew about five times faster than the population.
Part 4

Reaching the people who need it

Capacity is only useful if it reaches people. Here we follow preventive screenings for lower-income (B40) Malaysians, blood donations, and organ pledges — three signals of a system that reaches beyond hospital walls.

B40 health screenings per 1,000 people

Preventive screenings for lower-income Malaysians, 2019–2025. Filter by region, then click the legend to highlight a state.

COVID-19 left a clear dent in 2021 before screenings recovered.
Blood donations: 2012 vs. 2022

Donations per 10,000 people, by state, a decade apart.

Most states improved donation rates over the decade — but not all equally.
Organ pledges over time

Cumulative organ donation pledges in Malaysia, 2012–2022.

Pledges keep rising, but from a small base relative to population.
Part 5

Paying for it all

Behind every bed, every nurse and every screening is a budget. To close the story, we ask the simplest question of all: is Malaysia spending more on health?

Is Malaysia spending more on health?

Ministry of Health expenditure over time.

Spending is climbing — the real test is whether it grows faster than the demands placed on the system.
